Third Caliph · 23–35 AH · 644–656 CE
عُثْمَانُ بْنُ عَفَّانَ ذُو النُّورَيْنِ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

Uthman ibn Affan

The modest merchant of the Umayyad house who was among the first to believe, who married two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ one after the other, whom the angels were shy before, who bought a well for the thirsty and equipped an army for the Prophet ﷺ from his own purse, and who as caliph gave the Muslim world a single unified copy of the Quran. He was killed while reading it, refusing to shed a drop of blood in his own defence.

Dhu'n-Nurayn · Possessor of Two Lights, the Prophet's two daughters al-Ghani · the Generous Abu Amr · Abu Abdullah · his kunyahs Jami' al-Quran · Unifier of the Quran al-Shahid al-Mazlum · the wronged martyr

Section 1

Life at a glance

Wealthy, handsome, mild, generous and so modest that he bathed with his clothes on. He never drank wine or worshipped an idol before Islam, and he never missed a night of Quran after it.

576 CE
Birth
Son of Affan ibn Abi al-As and Arwa bint Kurayz, whose mother was the Prophet's paternal aunt al-Bayda. A leading merchant of Quraysh, loved by all for his gentleness.
610
Among the very first
Abu Bakr brought him to the Prophet ﷺ. He believed at once, and his uncle al-Hakam tied him up to make him recant; he said: "By Allah, I will never leave it."
c. 611
Marriage to Ruqayyah
The Prophet ﷺ gave him his daughter Ruqayyah. The women of Makkah sang: "The best couple anyone has seen: Ruqayyah and her husband Uthman."
615
Emigration to Abyssinia
The Prophet ﷺ said: "He is the first to migrate with his family for the sake of Allah since Lut" (al-Bayhaqi; Ibn Asakir). He returned and migrated again to Madinah.
624 · 2 AH
Badr: present in reward
Ruqayyah lay dying and the Prophet ﷺ told him to stay by her. She died the day the news of victory came. The Prophet ﷺ gave him a full share and said: "You have the reward and the share of one who was present at Badr" (Bukhari 3130).
625 · 3 AH
Umm Kulthum
The Prophet ﷺ married his second daughter Umm Kulthum to him, saying: "Had I a third, I would have married her to Uthman." She died in 9 AH. He became "Dhu'n-Nurayn".
625–627
Uhud, the Trench, the well of Rumah
He bought the only sweet well in Madinah from a Jewish owner for 35,000 dirhams and made it free for all. He then bought land to enlarge the Prophet's mosque.
628 · 6 AH
Envoy at Hudaybiyah
Sent into Makkah as the Prophet's ambassador. When it was rumoured he had been killed, the Pledge of Ridwan was taken; the Prophet ﷺ pledged on his behalf with his own hand.
630 · 9 AH
The Army of Hardship
For Tabuk he gave 950 camels, 50 horses and 1,000 dinars. The Prophet ﷺ turned the coins over in his lap and said: "Nothing Uthman does after today will harm him."
644 · 23 AH
Chosen by the Shura
Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf consulted every household in Madinah for three days and nights and found them inclining to Uthman. Ali was the first to pledge after him.
c. 650 · 25–30 AH
The unified Quran
He commissioned Zayd ibn Thabit and three Qurayshi scribes to copy the Quran from Abu Bakr's collection into a standard text, sent copies to the provinces and had variant sheets destroyed.
656 · 35 AH
The siege and martyrdom
Rebels from Egypt, Kufa and Basra besieged his house for forty days. He forbade the Companions to fight for him. On the Day of Sacrifice they broke in and killed him, aged 82, as he read the Quran; his blood fell on the verse "Allah will suffice you against them" (2:137).
Section 2

The Quran and Uthman

Two verses the commentators tie to him by name, and the pledge Allah was pleased with, made on his account.

Quran 39:9Ibn Abi Hatim · Ibn Asakir · al-Durr al-Manthur · Khaza'in al-Irfan
أَمَّنْ هُوَ قَانِتٌ آنَاءَ اللَّيْلِ سَاجِدًا وَقَائِمًا يَحْذَرُ الْآخِرَةَ وَيَرْجُو رَحْمَةَ رَبِّهِ ۗ قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِي الَّذِينَ يَعْلَمُونَ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"Is one who is devoutly obedient in the hours of the night, prostrating and standing, fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord…? Say: are those who know equal to those who do not know?" Ibn Umar said: "This was revealed about Uthman ibn Affan." He was famous for reciting the whole Quran in a single night of prayer; his wife Na'ilah said at his death: "You have killed a man who used to bring the whole Quran to life in one rak'ah."

Quran 48:10 · 48:18Bukhari 3698 · Tirmidhi 3702
إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُبَايِعُونَكَ إِنَّمَا يُبَايِعُونَ اللَّهَ يَدُ اللَّهِ فَوْقَ أَيْدِيهِمْ

"Those who pledge allegiance to you pledge allegiance only to Allah; the Hand of Allah is over their hands." The Pledge of Ridwan was taken because Uthman was reported killed in Makkah. The Prophet ﷺ struck his own right hand upon his left and said: "This is the hand of Uthman" and "This is for Uthman." Ibn Umar said to the men of Kufa: "The Messenger of Allah's hand for Uthman was better than Uthman's own hand for himself."

Quran 57:10
لَا يَسْتَوِي مِنكُم مَّنْ أَنفَقَ مِن قَبْلِ الْفَتْحِ وَقَاتَلَ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ أَعْظَمُ دَرَجَةً

"Not equal among you are those who spent before the Conquest and fought; they are greater in rank…" The commentators say Uthman is at the front of this verse: no Companion spent as much before the Conquest of Makkah as he did.

Quran 9:92al-Tabari · al-Baghawi
وَلَا عَلَى الَّذِينَ إِذَا مَا أَتَوْكَ لِتَحْمِلَهُمْ قُلْتَ لَا أَجِدُ مَا أَحْمِلُكُمْ عَلَيْهِ

The verse describes the believers who came weeping because the Prophet ﷺ had no mounts for them for Tabuk. It was Uthman who provided the mounts: the commentators record that the Prophet ﷺ raised his hands and said: "O Allah, be pleased with Uthman, for I am pleased with him."

Quran 2:137Ibn Sa'd · al-Tabari
فَسَيَكْفِيكَهُمُ اللَّهُ ۚ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْعَلِيمُ

"Allah will suffice you against them, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing." His blood fell upon these words as he was killed with the Quran open before him. The very Mushaf, stained, was preserved in Madinah for generations.

Quran 4:69Bukhari 3675
فَأُولَٰئِكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِم مِّنَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ وَالصَّالِحِينَ

"…with those Allah has blessed: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous." On Mount Uhud the Prophet ﷺ named the first three categories standing on it: "a Prophet, a Siddiq, and two martyrs." Uthman was one of the two.

Section 3

What the Prophet ﷺ said about him

Muslim 2401
أَلَا أَسْتَحِي مِنْ رَجُلٍ تَسْتَحِي مِنْهُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ؟

The Prophet ﷺ was reclining with his thigh uncovered when Abu Bakr and then Umar came in, and he stayed as he was. When Uthman asked permission, he sat up and covered himself. Aishah asked why. He said: "Should I not be shy of a man of whom the angels are shy?"

Bukhari 3695 · Muslim 2403
ائْذَنْ لَهُ وَبَشِّرْهُ بِالْجَنَّةِ عَلَى بَلْوَى تُصِيبُهُ

Abu Musa was the doorkeeper at the well. For Abu Bakr and Umar the Prophet ﷺ said "give him glad tidings of Paradise"; for Uthman: "Admit him and give him glad tidings of Paradise, with a trial that will befall him." Uthman said: "O Allah, patience," and "Allah is the one whose help is sought."

Tirmidhi 3705 · Ibn Majah 112 · Musnad Ahmad
يَا عُثْمَانُ، إِنَّهُ لَعَلَّ اللهَ يُقَمِّصُكَ قَمِيصًا، فَإِنْ أَرَادُوكَ عَلَى خَلْعِهِ فَلَا تَخْلَعْهُ لَهُمْ

"O Uthman, it may be that Allah will clothe you in a shirt. If they want you to take it off, do not take it off for them." Narrated by Aishah. Thirty years later, when the rebels demanded he abdicate, he replied: "I will not take off a shirt Allah has clothed me in."

Bukhari 2778 · Tirmidhi 3703
مَنْ يَحْفِرُ بِئْرَ رُومَةَ فَلَهُ الْجَنَّةُ، فَحَفَرْتُهَا. مَنْ جَهَّزَ جَيْشَ الْعُسْرَةِ فَلَهُ الْجَنَّةُ، فَجَهَّزْتُهُ

During the siege Uthman called to the crowd: "Do you know the Prophet ﷺ said: 'Whoever digs the well of Rumah will have Paradise', and I dug it? And that he said: 'Whoever equips the Army of Hardship will have Paradise', and I equipped it?" They confirmed it.

Tirmidhi 3701 · al-Hakim 3/102
مَا ضَرَّ عُثْمَانَ مَا عَمِلَ بَعْدَ الْيَوْمِ، مَا ضَرَّ عُثْمَانَ مَا عَمِلَ بَعْدَ الْيَوْمِ

Uthman poured a thousand dinars into the Prophet's lap for Tabuk. The Prophet ﷺ turned them over with his hand and said twice: "Nothing Uthman does after today will harm him."

Bukhari 3675
اثْبُتْ أُحُدُ، فَإِنَّمَا عَلَيْكَ نَبِيٌّ وَصِدِّيقٌ وَشَهِيدَانِ

"Be still, Uhud; upon you are a Prophet, a Siddiq and two martyrs." The Prophet ﷺ announced his martyrdom, and Umar's, decades before it happened.

Tirmidhi 3698 · Ibn Majah 109
لِكُلِّ نَبِيٍّ رَفِيقٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ، وَرَفِيقِي فِيهَا عُثْمَانُ بْنُ عَفَّانَ

"Every prophet has a close companion in Paradise, and my companion there is Uthman ibn Affan."

Bukhari 3130
إِنَّ لَكَ أَجْرَ رَجُلٍ مِمَّنْ شَهِدَ بَدْرًا وَسَهْمَهُ

"You have the reward of a man who was present at Badr, and his share." Said when Uthman stayed in Madinah to nurse the Prophet's daughter Ruqayyah. Ibn Umar used this hadith to answer those who tried to belittle Uthman for missing Badr.

al-Tabarani · Ibn Asakir · al-Riyad al-Nadirah
لَوْ كَانَتْ عِنْدِي ثَالِثَةٌ لَزَوَّجْتُهَا عُثْمَانَ

"Had I a third daughter, I would have married her to Uthman." Said after the death of Umm Kulthum. The Prophet ﷺ gave him two of his daughters; no other man was so honoured.

Tirmidhi 3704 · Musnad Ahmad
إِنَّ عُثْمَانَ رَجُلٌ حَيِيٌّ، وَإِنِّي خَشِيتُ إِنْ بَعَثْتُهُ أَنْ يَسْتَحْيِيَ فَيَرْجِعَ

"Uthman is a man of modesty; I feared that if I sent him he would be too shy and return." Even his shyness was noted by the Prophet ﷺ as a mark of his character. In another report: "The most truly modest of my community is Uthman" (Tirmidhi 3790, Ibn Majah 154).

Tirmidhi 3708 · al-Hakim 3/99
إِنَّهُمْ سَيُرِيدُونَ أَنْ تَخْلَعَ قَمِيصًا كَسَاكَهُ اللهُ… وَاللهِ لَا أَخْلَعُهُ

Al-Nu'man ibn Bashir reports from Aishah that the Prophet ﷺ told Uthman he would be asked to remove a shirt Allah had clothed him with, and Uthman answered: "By Allah, I will not remove it." Aishah said: "They forgot it, so I reminded them."

Section 4

The Companions on Uthman

Ali ibn Abi Talib

كَانَ عُثْمَانُ أَوْصَلَنَا لِلرَّحِمِ وَأَتْقَانَا لِلرَّبِّ

"Uthman was the most dutiful of us to family ties and the most God-fearing of us toward the Lord" (Ibn Asakir; al-Riyad al-Nadirah). Ali sent his sons Hasan and Husayn with swords to guard Uthman's door during the siege, sent water when the rebels cut it off, and when he was killed said: "O Allah, I did not kill him, and I did not approve." Ali also named Uthman third after Abu Bakr and Umar (Bukhari 3671 in its longer wording) and was first to pledge to him.

Ibn Umar

كُنَّا فِي زَمَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ لَا نَعْدِلُ بِأَبِي بَكْرٍ أَحَدًا، ثُمَّ عُمَرَ، ثُمَّ عُثْمَانَ، ثُمَّ نَتْرُكُ أَصْحَابَ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ لَا نُفَاضِلُ بَيْنَهُمْ

"In the time of the Prophet ﷺ we held no one equal to Abu Bakr, then Umar, then Uthman; then we left the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ without ranking them" (Bukhari 3697). When a man of Egypt tried to belittle Uthman for missing Badr, missing the Pledge of Ridwan and fleeing at Uhud, Ibn Umar answered each point with the Prophet's own words and said: "Take these with you now."

Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf

Charged with the choice, he went through Madinah for three days, asking even the women behind their curtains and the children going to school, and found them inclining to Uthman. He took his hand in the mosque and said: "I pledge to you on the Sunnah of Allah and His Messenger and the two caliphs after him."

Zayd ibn Thabit

The scribe of the Prophet ﷺ who wrote the Mushaf for Abu Bakr and again for Uthman: "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: 'Uthman passed by me while an angel was with me, and the angel said: A martyr whom his people will kill; we are shy of him.'" (Musnad Ahmad; Ibn Asakir)

Hasan ibn Ali and the Companions

Hasan ibn Ali, Abdullah ibn Umar, Abdullah ibn Zubayr and Abu Hurayrah stood at his door in arms; he told them: "I hold whoever has a right over me to obedience: put down your weapons; I do not want a drop of blood spilled for me." Abu Hurayrah said: "By Allah, the killing of Uthman is a calamity for all who come after." Hudhayfah: "The first of the trials was the killing of Uthman."

Aishah and Umm Salamah

Aishah: "They killed him after they had wrung him out as a garment is wrung, and he was innocent." Umm Salamah: "By Allah, he was killed wrongfully, and the Prophet ﷺ was pleased with him."

Section 5

Twelve years, one Book

The Muslim world grew larger under Uthman than under any caliph before him; but his greatest deed fits in the hand: the Mushaf.

The unified Quran

Hudhayfah returned from Armenia alarmed that Muslims in Syria and Iraq were disputing over readings. Uthman borrowed the sheets from Hafsah, appointed Zayd ibn Thabit with Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr, Sa'id ibn al-As and Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith, ordered them to write in the dialect of Quraysh, sent a copy with a reciter to Makkah, Kufa, Basra, Damascus, and later Yemen and Bahrain, and had all other sheets burned. Every printed Quran on earth descends from these copies. Ali said: "Do not say anything about Uthman except good, for by Allah he did what he did with the Mushafs only after consulting all of us" (Ibn Abi Dawud, al-Masahif). (Bukhari 4987)

The first navy

Umar had refused to let Muslims sail. Uthman permitted Mu'awiyah to build a fleet; Cyprus was opened in 28 AH, and in 34 AH the Battle of the Masts destroyed the Byzantine fleet, the largest naval battle of its age. Umm Haram bint Milhan, whom the Prophet ﷺ had promised she would be among the first to sail in the way of Allah, was buried in Cyprus.

Expansion

Armenia, Azerbaijan and the Caucasus in the east; Khorasan and the borders of Sind; Tripoli, Tunisia and the whole of North Africa under Abdullah ibn Sa'd; the Persian empire finally extinguished with the death of Yazdegerd III in 31 AH.

The two mosques

He enlarged the Prophet's mosque in 29 AH with carved stone pillars and a teak roof, and enlarged the Sacred Mosque in Makkah. He was the first to add the first call to prayer on Friday, needed because the city had grown (Bukhari 912).

Public works and charity

Raised the stipends of the Diwan by a quarter, ordered the governors to feed the people in Ramadan, dug wells and built rest-houses on the roads, established a police force (shurtah) and granted land to cultivators. He took no salary from the treasury; he fed the people from his own wealth and ate vinegar and oil at home.

The rebellion

In his later years, agitators led by Abdullah ibn Saba spread complaints about the governors, most of whom he had inherited or appointed on merit. Bands from Egypt, Kufa and Basra came to Madinah in 35 AH. He answered every complaint, promised reforms and dismissed governors; they returned with a forged letter and besieged his house.

The last day

He saw the Prophet ﷺ in a dream with Abu Bakr and Umar: "Break your fast with us tonight." He fasted, freed his slaves, put on his trousers so his body would be covered, and sat with the Quran. Al-Ghafiqi struck him with an iron bar; Na'ilah threw herself over him and lost her fingers to a sword. He was 82. He had ruled twelve years without shedding blood in his own cause.

His words in the siege

"I have heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say the blood of a Muslim is lawful only for three things: disbelief after faith, adultery after marriage, or a life for a life. By Allah, I have not committed adultery in the time of ignorance or Islam, I have not apostatised since I pledged to Allah's Messenger, and I have not killed a soul. So for what would you kill me?"
Source: Tirmidhi 2158; Abu Dawud 4502; Nasa'i 4019.
Section 6

Character and sayings

Generosity and modesty were his two wings. He fed the community on the finest food and ate oil and vinegar at home; he slept in the mosque on a straw mat with the marks on his side.

"If our hearts were pure, we would never have our fill of the words of our Lord."
Abu Nu'aym, Hilyat al-Awliya; al-Suyuti, Tarikh al-Khulafa.
"I would hate for a day to pass in which I did not look into the Book of Allah."
Ibn Sa'd; Abu Nu'aym.
"Allah restrains by the ruler what He does not restrain by the Quran."
Ibn Abd al-Barr; al-Suyuti, Tarikh al-Khulafa.
"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: 'The best of you is the one who learns the Quran and teaches it.'"
His narration, Bukhari 5027. He taught the Quran in the mosque for years.
"Preoccupation with this world is darkness in the heart; preoccupation with the next is light."
Abu Nu'aym, Hilyat al-Awliya.
"If I were between Paradise and the Fire, not knowing to which I would be sent, I would choose to be ashes before I knew."
Abu Nu'aym, Hilyat al-Awliya.
"Whoever loves me loves the Prophet ﷺ; for I was raised in his house and married in his house."
Reported by his household. Ibn Asakir.
"O Allah, unite this community."
His prayer under siege. Al-Tabari.

His weeping at graves

He wept so much at a grave that his beard became wet. Asked why he wept at graves and not when the Fire was mentioned, he said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said the grave is the first station of the Hereafter; whoever is saved there, what follows is easier; whoever is not, what follows is harder" (Tirmidhi 2308).

His family

Wives: Ruqayyah and Umm Kulthum, daughters of the Prophet ﷺ; later Fakhitah, Umm Amr, Fatimah bint al-Walid, Umm al-Banin, Ramlah and Na'ilah. Sons: Abdullah (from Ruqayyah, died in childhood, buried by the Prophet ﷺ), Amr, Aban (the historian), Umar, Khalid, Sa'id, al-Walid; and daughters.

Section 7

Scholars through the ages

Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal

"The Companions were unanimous on the pledge to Uthman; anyone who belittles him has belittled the consensus of the Companions."

Al-Hasan al-Basri

He lived through the caliphate as a young man in Madinah: "I saw Uthman sleeping in the mosque with his cloak under his head, and the people sitting around him as if he were one of them. In his time a man would fear no one but Allah." And: "The years of Uthman were the years of plenty; the caller would call: come to your stipends, come to your provisions."

Al-Dhahabi

"Amir al-Mu'minin, Dhu'n-Nurayn, the martyr, one of the Ten, one of the six of the Shura, one of the first to believe, the husband of two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ, the unifier of the Book. His virtues are many, and he was killed wronged."

Ibn Kathir

In al-Bidayah: "The correct view is that Uthman's rank is after Umar and before Ali; that is the view of the majority of the community and of the Companions who lived in Madinah, as Ibn Umar reported."

Al-Suyuti

In Tarikh al-Khulafa he collects the prophecies of Uthman's martyrdom and writes: "No one after him did what he did for the Book of Allah; every reciter and every listener owes him a debt."

Imam Ahmad Raza Khan

He calls him "Uthman-e-Ghani", the Generous, and in Fatawa Ridawiyyah treats the consensus of the Companions upon his caliphate as decisive, citing Ali's own pledge to him and his sending of Hasan and Husayn to guard him. He describes Uthman's modesty as the modesty the Prophet ﷺ himself praised, and his martyrdom as a martyrdom foretold by the Prophet ﷺ, so that whoever speaks against him speaks against a prophecy.

His note in Khaza'in al-Irfan on verse 39:9 records Ibn Umar's statement that it was revealed about Uthman, and on 48:10 records the Prophet's hand pledging on Uthman's behalf.

Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani

"The Mushaf of Uthman is the greatest of the deeds by which the community has been protected; its blessing is on every tongue that recites."
